**1. States vs. Prediction Markets: The Fight Over the Meaning of 'Swap'**
Shreyas Hariharan
https://www.dopaminemarkets.com/p/prediction-markets-vs-states-the

The legal fight that will define the industry. Courts are split across 19 federal lawsuits over whether sports event contracts are "swaps" under the Commodity Exchange Act, which would give the CFTC exclusive jurisdiction and preempt state gambling laws. The Third Circuit ruled in Kalshi's favor. The case hinges on two phrases in Dodd-Frank and will likely reach the Supreme Court within two years.

**2. Truth Machines Go to War**
Matt Levine (@matt_levine)
https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-04-06/truth-machines-go-to-war

Prediction markets need binary boundaries, but reality doesn't provide them. Levine uses Kalshi's "mentions markets" (did Powell say a specific word?) to show the problem: did Cardi B "perform" at the Super Bowl? Did Zelenskiy "wear a suit"? Are singular and plural nouns the same word? Platforms need linguists and philosophers as much as traders.

**3. Seeing Like a Market: Event Contracts and Market Topology**
Lauris Marinson
https://www.slampaper.xyz/

When do prediction markets become structurally cheaper than derivatives for pricing binary risk? This paper analyzes 87 contracts across 11 categories and finds high-VRP categories like Bitcoin and elections already cross the displacement threshold. FOMC markets compressed from a 12pp cost gap to under 2pp between 2024 and 2026. The cost difference is "apparatus rent" paid for dealer infrastructure that event contracts can bypass.

**4. Leverage in Prediction Markets**
@0xMims
https://x.com/0xMims/status/2041498106181627918

Landscape report comparing four models for leverage: lending pools, prime brokers, synthetic desks, and perpetual futures. Sizes the opportunity at $15M base to $50.7M bull case, with 87% driven by financing revenue on open interest. All four models share a structural dependency on CLOB venue architecture that degrades during jump events. Includes a Kalshi case study where a single adverse selection fill cost $21,384 in twenty minutes.

**5. The Two Kinds of Prediction Markets**
@4castersbet
https://x.com/4castersbet/status/2042268072266920213

Applies the sportsbook square vs. sharp distinction to prediction markets. Kalshi (3.5% take fee) and Polymarket (1.5%+) are building square prediction markets that monetize price-insensitive retail. Sharp prediction markets focused on trading efficiency will thrive outside the US. 4casters raised fees from 0.5% to 0.75% with no material volume impact, confirming sports bettors are less price-sensitive than assumed.

**6. How Prediction Markets Are Blurring the Line Between Trading and Betting**
Christopher Beam
https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-09/how-prediction-markets-are-blurring-the-line-between-trading-and-betting

Bloomberg Businessweek feature on the collision between prediction markets and state gambling regulators. As billions in volume attract enforcement actions from multiple states and insider trading concerns mount, the central question sharpens: are these platforms financial infrastructure or disguised betting operations? Covers partnerships with CNN, CNBC, and major sports leagues.
