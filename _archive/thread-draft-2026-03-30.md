From the microstructure of who wins and loses, to when crowds get it right, to how to actually use the data, to where the whole category might be headed.

**1. The Microstructure of Wealth Transfer in Prediction Markets**
Jonathan Becker
https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure

Analyzes 72.1 million trades and $18.26 billion in volume on Kalshi. Documents a systematic wealth transfer from takers to makers averaging 1.12% excess returns per side. Takers disproportionately buy YES longshots, accepting returns 64 percentage points lower than equivalent NO positions. This pattern only appeared after Kalshi's October 2024 legal victory attracted professional algorithmic market makers. Efficiency varies sharply by category: finance markets are near-efficient, entertainment and media markets show gaps exceeding 7 percentage points.

**2. Decomposing Crowd Wisdom: Domain-Specific Calibration Dynamics in Prediction Markets**
Nam Anh Le
https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19520

Fits a Bayesian hierarchical model to 292 million trades across 327,000 contracts on Kalshi and Polymarket. Decomposes calibration errors into three components: universal horizon effects, domain-specific biases, and trade-size scale effects — together explaining 87.3% of variance on Kalshi. Political markets show persistent underconfidence, with prices compressing toward 50%. Large trades amplify this on Kalshi but not Polymarket, pointing to platform-specific microstructure differences that matter more than most people assume.

**3. How to Use Prediction Markets as a High Quality Info Source**
Isar Bhattacharjee
https://uncover.substack.com/p/how-to-use-prediction-markets-as

A practical guide for people who consume prediction market data but don't trade. Proposes a tiered framework for reliability: financialized economic indicators rank highest, speculative prop bets lowest. Outlines three use cases — triangulating against traditional polls, nowcasting delayed economic data in real time, and hedging event risk. Draws on a Federal Reserve paper validating Kalshi's data quality and Tetlock's forecasting research.

**4. The Probability Layers Are Coming**
@BlondiePredicts
https://x.com/BlondiePredicts/status/2038595927225622569

Steps back from trading and asks: what if prediction markets are just the proof of concept? Proposes three "probability layers" that extend market mechanisms beyond trading — attention markets that price virality forward instead of measuring it backward, credibility markets that turn trust into a live score instead of a static label, and demand markets that capture consumer intent before production. The argument is that probability signals will eventually embed into every decision surface on the internet, invisible as infrastructure.
