4 new reads added to the On Prediction library this week.

Each one digs into who's actually trading on prediction markets, how they behave, and what the data says when you look past the surface.

**1. From Iran to Taylor Swift: Informed Trading in Prediction Markets**
Joshua Mitts & Moran Ofir
https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2026/03/25/from-iran-to-taylor-swift-informed-trading-in-prediction-markets/

The first systematic empirical study of insider trading on prediction markets. Screens 93,000 Polymarket markets and flags wallets with a 69.9% win rate — more than 60 standard deviations above chance. Estimates $143 million in anomalous profits across cases ranging from military strikes to celebrity announcements. Then maps the legal gaps: securities law doesn't apply, CFTC rules are too narrow, and wire fraud statutes don't fit either. Proposes a three-part regulatory framework to close them.

**2. The Yes Bias Might Not Exist**
@functionspaceHQ
https://x.com/functionspaceHQ/article/2037374271278989409

Challenges one of the most widely accepted claims in the space. Pulls resolution data for 7,292 Polymarket markets (59% resolve NO) and trade data for 88 high-volume events. The finding: traders don't prefer YES. They prefer cheap. When you control for token price, small and large traders buy at similar YES/NO ratios. The apparent yes bias is longshot preference channeled through Polymarket's "Will X happen?" question framing, which systematically makes YES the cheap token. Would need randomized labeling to prove definitively.

**3. Prediction Markets: The Next Level**
@0xkeshav_
https://x.com/0xkeshav_/article/1970163909224169626

What if you could borrow against your prediction market positions instead of locking up capital until resolution? This piece walks through how position collateralization could fix liquidity in long-dated markets, correct persistent mispricings by making arbitrage capital-efficient, and open composability with the rest of DeFi. Also honest about the hard parts: binary outcomes are nearly impossible to hedge, and liquidation cascades on event-driven price moves are a real risk.

**4. Is Polymarket a Retail Product or a Pro Trading Venue?**
@sealaunch_
https://x.com/sealaunch_/article/2037228250116522082

Categorizes every active Polymarket wallet by trade frequency and volume into seven personas. The headline number: 2% of users generate 90% of all platform volume. Crypto markets are dominated by algorithmic execution. Sports attract a broader mix of serious but non-automated bettors. Politics drives the most users but mostly casual, event-driven ones who may never come back. The category you launch determines which persona you attract — and that shapes everything from liquidity depth to fee tolerance.
