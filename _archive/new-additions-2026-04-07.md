**1. Who Profits from Prediction Markets? Execution, Not Information**
Joshua Della Vedova
https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6191618

Analyzes 222 million Polymarket trades and decomposes returns into two independent skill dimensions: directional accuracy and execution quality. Traders with above-random forecasting accuracy earn negative returns because they arrive late and pay unfavorable prices. Automated traders with near-random directional skill profit by paying 2.52 cents less per contract. The two dimensions are nearly orthogonal — being a good forecaster and being a profitable trader are fundamentally different things.

**2. Parimutuel Prediction Markets**
@meleemarkets
https://x.com/meleemarkets/status/2041244791716110409

Traces the parimutuel model from Joseph Oller's 1867 invention to its potential in modern prediction markets. Parimutuel pools bootstrap liquidity from dollar one without market makers and are genuinely peer-to-peer. But the raw model has friction points for continuous markets: locked positions, timing exploits by late bettors, and prices that don't read as probabilities. The architecture is right but needs an upgrade.

**3. The Financialization of Uncertainty**
@kaviish
https://x.com/kaviish/status/2041214800731033700

Industry landscape mapping $63.5B in 2025 volume to a $200B+ 2026 run rate. Sports drive 83% of Kalshi's volume, but $22B valuations price in an information infrastructure future that hasn't arrived yet. Distribution platforms like Robinhood and Coinbase are vertically integrating into exchange infrastructure, shifting the moat from licensing to owning the user. The central tension: prediction markets are valued as financial infrastructure but still monetized as sports products.
