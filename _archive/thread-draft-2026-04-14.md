**1. Faster, Shorter, More Automated: Anatomy of Polymarket's Fastest Markets**
Dune (@Dune)
https://x.com/Dune/status/2044045952730726863

Seven months of on-chain data on Polymarket's fast crypto markets. 5-minute contracts overtook 15-minute in weeks ($2.3B vs $795M in notional volume), bots control 55-62% of volume in the fastest markets, and Bitcoin drives 77% of turnover. With $23.7M in taker fees collected in 83 days through a maker-rebate flywheel, Dune argues Polymarket has crossed from prediction market experiment into derivatives exchange territory.

**2. Why AMMs Failed Prediction Markets**
Melee (@meleemarkets)
https://x.com/meleemarkets/status/2043766417342755259

Historical walkthrough of why LMSR-based AMMs structurally failed for prediction markets even as they powered DeFi. In a binary market that resolves to 0 or 1, impermanent loss becomes permanent: every pool eventually holds a pile of worthless shares on the losing side. Polymarket figured this out early and migrated to a central limit order book in late 2022, cementing CLOB as the dominant architecture for prediction market liquidity.

**3. Are We on the Brink of a Prediction Market Supercycle?**
mph (@mphrediction)
https://x.com/mphrediction/status/2043419029378048164

Builder's bullish case for why prediction markets are still early. Argues the absence of cult-like community formation (compared to NFTs or meme-coins), nascent builders programs, and upcoming permissionless markets all point to a supercycle ahead. Covers the gambling-vs-trading distinction, insider trading as both a problem and a signal source, and why Polymarket offers better odds than traditional sportsbooks on most events.

**4. Polymarket Is Not a Truth Machine**
Vaidik Mandloi (@vaidikmandloi)
https://www.thetokendispatch.com/p/polymarket-is-not-a-truth-machine

The counter-take. Polymarket's headline Brier score of 0.047 masks category-specific failures: sports markets score 0.325, worse than a coin flip, and individual contracts like Harris-Dem-nominee hit 0.9098 despite resolving correctly. 99% of volume concentrates in the final hours before resolution, and Mandloi argues prediction markets only work on about 2% of listed contracts (binary, high-profile, short-term events with millions at stake). When CNN and WSJ broadcast thin-market odds as authoritative signal, whale trades on illiquid books get pre-laundered through credible newsrooms.
