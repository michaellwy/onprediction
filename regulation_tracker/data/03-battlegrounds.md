# Battlegrounds — Prediction Markets Global Regulation Tracker

Key regulatory debates and fault lines. One structured block per battleground.

## BG-01 | Federal Preemption vs. State Authority

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| core_tension | Does the CEA give the CFTC exclusive jurisdiction over event contracts, or can states regulate PMs under gambling law? |
| side_a | **Pro-preemption:** CFTC (Chairman Selig), Kalshi, Polymarket, Coinbase, Robinhood, Coalition for PMs. Argue CEA grants exclusive federal jurisdiction over event contracts as swaps/derivatives. CFTC filed amicus in 9th Circuit. |
| side_b | **Anti-preemption:** 38 state AGs (amicus supporting MD), state gaming commissions, sportsbook lobby, Mick Mulvaney / "Gambling Is Not Investing." Argue states retain regulatory authority over gambling regardless of federal derivative classification. |
| key_cases | Kalshi v. CFTC (D.C., won on election contracts) [KalshiEX LLC v. CFTC, No. 24-5205 (D.C. Cir.)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/cadc/24-5205/24-5205-2024-10-02.html) (2024-10-02); Kalshi v. NJ (won, preemption upheld, 3d Cir. appeal); Kalshi v. MD (lost, 4th Cir. appeal); MA v. Kalshi (injunction granted 2026-01-20); NV dissolved injunction Dec 2025; CFTC amicus in 9th Circuit [CFTC PR 9183-26](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9183-26) (2026-02-17) |
| current_status | Circuit split. 19+ federal lawsuits across multiple circuits. Pro-preemption win in NJ (on appeal to 3d Cir.); anti-preemption wins in MA/MD/NV(reversed Nov 2025)/OH. CT issued C&D orders but Kalshi won temporary relief. 36-38 state AGs filed amicus briefs supporting state authority in multiple circuits. CFTC actively intervening via amicus briefs. |
| trajectory | Heading to appellate courts and likely Supreme Court within 12-24 months. Congress may legislate first via PM Security and Integrity Act (which would reverse preemption) or CFTC ANPRM process. |
| industry_impact | Platforms cannot operate nationally without resolution. State-by-state compliance is prohibitively expensive. New entrants frozen until jurisdictional question settled. |
| resolution_scenarios | (1) Supreme Court rules on preemption — 2+ years. (2) Congress passes explicit federal framework via CFTC ANPRM. (3) PM Security and Integrity Act reverses preemption. (4) Fragmented state-by-state regime persists. |

## BG-02 | Gambling vs. Derivatives Classification

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| core_tension | Are prediction markets gambling or financial derivatives? Classification determines regulator, rules, tax treatment, and legitimacy. |
| side_a | **Derivatives:** CFTC, Kalshi, exchanges (CME, Cboe). Event contracts are swaps under CEA. Used for hedging and price discovery. Fed FEDS paper validated forecasting quality. |
| side_b | **Gambling:** State AGs, sportsbook operators, Mulvaney. Sports contracts are "indistinguishable" from wagers (MD ruling). 90% of volume is sports. "If it looks like a sports bet..." |
| key_cases | MD ruling: sports contracts = wagering; NV ruling: parlays/props not swaps; D.C.: election contracts not "gaming"; NJ: event contracts are swaps |
| current_status | No consensus. Different courts reaching different conclusions on same question. CFTC ANPRM seeks to establish clear federal framework. Classification varies by contract type (sports vs. economic vs. political). |
| trajectory | Sports contracts most likely to be classified as gambling; economic/political contracts more likely to survive as derivatives. Bifurcated framework emerging. |
| industry_impact | Classification determines who regulates (CFTC vs. state gaming), what license needed, tax rate, age requirements, and consumer protections. |

## BG-03 | Sports Betting Overlap

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| core_tension | PMs are siphoning $600M+ in state sports betting tax revenue. States argue sports event contracts are unlicensed sports bets. |
| side_a | **Pro-PM sports:** Kalshi (90% sports volume), Polymarket. Argue sports contracts are derivatives not bets. Point to federal DCM authorization. |
| side_b | **Anti-PM sports:** State AGs, licensed sportsbooks (DraftKings, FanDuel), AGA. $600M+ lost tax revenue. 11 states issued cease-and-desist orders. Licensed operators paying 15-20% tax while PMs pay zero. |
| current_status | Most active battleground. 11 states issued C&D orders. Kalshi took $1.9B in college basketball wagers in Feb 2026 alone. KY HB 904 bans sportsbook-PM partnerships. Moore-Carbajal bill would give states opt-out on sports contracts. |
| trajectory | Sports contracts face highest regulatory risk. Even pro-PM lawmakers (Moore) support state authority over sports. Likely outcome: sports PMs either banned, taxed at parity with sportsbooks, or subject to state opt-in. |
| industry_impact | Sports is 87-90% of Kalshi and 38% of Polymarket volume. Losing sports contracts would devastate PM revenue models. |

## BG-04 | Insider Trading & National Security

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| core_tension | Can people profit from classified information via PMs? Should PMs on war, terrorism, and death exist? Bipartisan concern. |
| side_a | **Pro-regulation:** Bipartisan Congress (Moore, Merkley, Blumenthal, Schiff), state AGs, Governor Newsom. Iran strikes scandal proved the risk. |
| side_b | **Self-regulation sufficient:** Kalshi (200 investigations, 2 referrals), Polymarket (Palantir surveillance partnership). Voluntary rules already in place. |
| key_cases | Iran strikes ($515K suspicious trade, Feb 2026); Maduro ouster ($400K+ suspicious trade, Jan 2026); Israeli arrests of insiders |
| current_status | 3+ Congressional bills targeting insider trading (S. 4017, H.R. 7004, PM Security Act). CA Governor signed EO. No existing federal insider trading law for event contracts. |
| trajectory | Insider trading legislation most likely to pass — bipartisan support. Question is scope: narrow (officials only) vs. broad (all MNPI). |
| industry_impact | If broad insider trading rules pass, could affect market liquidity and discourage informed trading (the mechanism that makes PMs valuable). |

## BG-05 | Market Listing Standards & Repugnance

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| core_tension | What should and shouldn't be a prediction market? Who decides? Death/terrorism/assassination markets create moral hazard and perverse incentives. |
| side_a | **Restrict:** Moore-Carbajal Event Contract Enforcement Act requires CFTC to prohibit TAWGA contracts (terrorism, assassination, war, gaming, illegal activity) + elections + government activity. |
| side_b | **Allow with guardrails:** CFTC ANPRM exploring which contracts may be "contrary to public interest" vs. allowed. Self-regulation approach. |
| current_status | CEA § 5c(c)(5)(C) already lists TAWGA categories but CFTC not required to enforce. Moore-Carbajal would mandate enforcement and add elections/government activity. |
| trajectory | TAWGA enforcement mandate likely to pass. Election/government contracts more contested — some argue they serve legitimate informational purpose. |

## BG-06 | Self-Regulation vs. Mandated Oversight

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| core_tension | Are platform voluntary rules sufficient, or does Congress need to mandate specific oversight? |
| side_a | **Self-regulation:** Kalshi (200 investigations, 2 referrals), Polymarket (Palantir partnership). DCMs already self-regulatory organizations supervised by CFTC. |
| side_b | **Mandated oversight:** Congress (PM Security Act), bipartisan senators. Self-regulation insufficient — Iran/Maduro scandals occurred despite voluntary rules. |
| current_status | CFTC staff advisory (9193-26) reminded DCMs of existing regulatory obligations. ANPRM exploring enhanced oversight. |
| trajectory | Enhanced mandatory oversight likely — question is whether via CFTC rulemaking or Congressional legislation. |

## BG-07 | Crypto Infrastructure & Regulatory Arbitrage

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| core_tension | Polymarket runs on Polygon blockchain. MiCA constrains crypto infrastructure in EU without addressing PMs. Offshore platforms evade national regulation. |
| side_a | **Crypto-native:** Polymarket. Decentralized infrastructure enables global access. Users bypass national bans via VPN. |
| side_b | **Regulators:** EU (MiCA CASP deadline July 2026), national gambling regulators blocking access. India using DPI to block smart contract endpoints. |
| current_status | MiCA grandfathering ends July 2026. 8+ EU countries banned Polymarket. India blocking at DPI level. Taiwan prosecuted users. Gap between regulation on paper and in practice. |
| trajectory | Technical enforcement growing more sophisticated (DPI, smart contract blocking). MiCA deadline will force platforms to either get licensed or exit EU. |

## BG-08 | Cross-Border Enforcement

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| core_tension | A user in France trades on Polymarket (Polygon chain, no KYC for non-US). France bans it. Can they actually enforce? |
| side_a | **Enforceable:** ISP blocks (SG, AU, TW, AR), DPI (India), app store restrictions, prosecution of users (TW — 17 arrested). |
| side_b | **Unenforceable in practice:** VPN bypass widespread (India remains top user base despite ban), decentralized infrastructure, no KYC for non-US users. |
| current_status | 30+ countries restrict Polymarket. Enforcement ranges from ISP blocks (most common) to user prosecution (Taiwan) to DPI (India). VPN usage persists everywhere. |
| trajectory | Increasingly aggressive enforcement tools. But fundamental tension between decentralized crypto infrastructure and national jurisdiction remains unresolved. |

## BG-09 | Consumer Protection & Market Integrity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| core_tension | What protections do PM users need? Wash trading, manipulation, addiction, KYC/AML, age verification, loss limits. |
