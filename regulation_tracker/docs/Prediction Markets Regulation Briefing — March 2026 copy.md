# Prediction Markets Regulation Briefing — March 2026

Research across X, web sources, and prediction market data. Covers the last 30 days of regulatory developments.

## Federal Regulatory Action

The [[CFTC]] issued an Advanced Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM) on March 12, 2026, formally opening the public comment process on what a federal regulatory framework for [[prediction markets]] should look like. Comments are due **April 30, 2026**. Key issues under consideration:

- Whether platforms can offer trading on margin
- What types of contracts should be prohibited on public interest grounds
- Whether individuals with insider knowledge should be barred from trading on related contracts

CFTC Chairman Michael Selig withdrew a proposed ban on political and sports event contracts, signaling a pro-market but guardrails-first approach. Applications to register as designated contract markets (DCMs) have more than doubled over the past year, with the majority from entities wanting to operate as prediction markets.

The [[CFTC]] also designated prediction markets as "swaps," placing them under exclusive federal jurisdiction — a move that directly challenges state-level gambling regulators.

## Congressional Legislation

**DEATH BETS Act** (introduced March 10, 2026) — amends the CEA to explicitly prohibit CFTC-registered platforms from listing contracts involving terrorism, assassination, war, or death.

**Prediction Markets Security and Integrity Act** — targets insider trading, enhances customer protections, and would explicitly reverse the CFTC's claimed preemption of state gambling regulations. Sens. Adam Schiff (D-CA) and John Curtis (R-UT) are pushing this even after [[Kalshi]] and [[Polymarket]] added self-imposed insider trading rules.

## State-Level Response

Eleven states have introduced prediction market legislation in 2026:

- **Hawaii** — moving toward a full ban
- **Kentucky** — passed a bill imposing a 17.25% tax on operator transaction fees
- **Washington** — AG filed a lawsuit against [[Kalshi]] on March 28, alleging violation of state gambling regulations
- **Massachusetts** — a judge ruled [[Kalshi]]'s sports event contracts are subject to state gaming laws, issuing a preliminary injunction
- **Nevada & New Jersey** — federal courts sided with [[Kalshi]], finding the CEA preempts state gambling law

States argue they're losing over **$600M in sports betting tax revenue** to prediction markets operating outside state gambling frameworks.

## The Federal vs. State Showdown

This is the defining legal question. [[Kalshi]] won preemption cases in Nevada and New Jersey (federal law trumps state gambling regs) but lost in Massachusetts. The split across jurisdictions is heading toward a major legal confrontation — likely reaching appellate courts or requiring Congressional resolution.

The Prediction Markets Security and Integrity Act would explicitly reverse CFTC preemption, handing power back to states.

## Platform Updates

**[[Polymarket]]** resumed limited US operations through a registered intermediary in late 2025, and has self-certified new market rules with the CFTC as of March 2026. Argentina blocked [[Polymarket]] access entirely, citing gambling regulations and user protection concerns.

**[[Kalshi]]** faces lawsuits from multiple state AGs while simultaneously winning federal preemption cases in other states. The platform is CFTC-approved as a Designated Contract Market.

## Integrity & Insider Trading

Israeli authorities arrested two people for using classified military information to profit on [[Polymarket]] — the exact scenario fueling Congressional action on "death bets" and insider trading. Both [[Kalshi]] and [[Polymarket]] added voluntary insider trading restrictions, but bipartisan senators say self-regulation isn't sufficient.

California's Governor Newsom signed an executive order targeting insider trading in prediction markets — signaling states will act unilaterally even without federal legislation.
